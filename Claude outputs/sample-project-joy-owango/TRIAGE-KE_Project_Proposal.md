## 2. Research Details

**Research areas:** Digital Health · Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare · Emergency Medicine · Health Systems Research · Implementation Science

### Abstract

Emergency departments (EDs) in Kenyan county referral hospitals manage high patient volumes with limited senior clinical staff, and triage accuracy varies widely. Under-triage, where a critically ill patient is assigned a lower priority than warranted, delays life-saving care. TRIAGE-KE will implement and evaluate an AI-assisted triage decision-support tool that suggests a South African Triage Scale (SATS) category from routinely collected vital signs, age and presenting complaint. The tool advises; the triage nurse always makes the final decision. Using a mixed-methods design across three county referral hospitals (Kisumu, Nakuru and Machakos counties), we will compare triage accuracy, under-triage and over-triage rates and time-to-triage during a 3-month baseline period and a 6-month implementation period, using interrupted time-series analysis. Clinician acceptability and implementation barriers will be explored through surveys (n≈60) and key-informant interviews (n≈30). Findings will inform county and national guidance on the safe adoption of AI decision support in emergency care.

### Research Objectives

**General objective:** To evaluate the effect, acceptability and implementation of an AI-assisted triage decision-support tool in EDs of Kenyan county referral hospitals.

**Specific objectives:**

1. To determine the effect of the tool on triage accuracy (agreement with an expert reference standard) compared with standard SATS triage.
2. To measure changes in under-triage and over-triage rates.
3. To assess the effect on time from ED arrival to triage completion.
4. To assess clinician usability and acceptability of the tool.
5. To identify barriers and enablers to sustained use using the Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research (CFIR).

### Methodology

- **Design:** Multi-site, mixed-methods, pre–post implementation study with interrupted time-series (ITS) analysis.
- **Setting:** Emergency departments of three county referral hospitals in Kisumu, Nakuru and Machakos counties.
- **Phases:**
  - *Phase 0 — Preparation (Oct–Nov 2025):* local calibration of the model on 12 months of retrospective de-identified triage records; DPIA; staff training.
  - *Phase 1 — Baseline (Nov 2025–Jan 2026):* standard SATS triage; prospective data collection.
  - *Phase 2 — Implementation (Feb–Jul 2026):* the tool runs on tablets at triage desks; nurses see a suggested category with key contributing features.
  - *Phase 3 — Analysis & dissemination (Aug 2026–Mar 2027).*
- **Population and sample:** All patients aged ≥ 12 years presenting to the ED during study hours. A systematic random sample of ~600 encounters per site per phase (target ≈ 3,600 encounters) gives 90% power to detect a 6-percentage-point reduction in under-triage (from 18% to 12%) at α = 0.05, allowing for clustering by site.
- **Reference standard:** Independent, blinded re-triage by a panel of two emergency physicians using full chart information, with a third to adjudicate disagreements.
- **Outcomes:**
  - *Primary:* under-triage rate.
  - *Secondary:* over-triage rate, weighted Cohen's κ against reference, median arrival-to-triage time, System Usability Scale (SUS) score.
- **Analysis:** Segmented regression for ITS (weekly data points); mixed-effects logistic regression with site as a random effect; thematic analysis of interviews (framework approach, CFIR-coded) in NVivo.
- **Data management:** REDCap on in-country cloud hosting; pseudonymised study IDs; linkage key held separately by the data manager.

## 5. Ethical Considerations

### Overview

The study is classed as **low risk**. The AI tool is advisory only: the triage nurse keeps full clinical responsibility and may accept or override every suggestion, and all overrides are logged. No change is made to treatment protocols. The main risks are (a) potential automation bias, which leads to wrong triage, and (b) breach of confidentiality of health records. These are mitigated by training on automation bias, weekly safety reviews of discordant cases by the clinical lead, a pre-specified stopping rule if under-triage rises more than 5 percentage points over baseline for two consecutive weeks, and the data-security measures below.

### Consent Procedures

- **Patient records:** A waiver of individual consent was approved for de-identified record abstraction because the tool is advisory, the risk is minimal, and seeking consent at triage would delay care. Notices are displayed in English and Kiswahili at every ED, and patients may opt out.
- **Clinicians (survey and interviews):** Written informed consent is obtained, with an information sheet in English and Kiswahili. Participation is voluntary and has no bearing on employment.
- **Minors (12–17 years):** Records only, under the same waiver. No minors are interviewed.

### Data Security Measures

- Compliance with the Kenya Data Protection Act (2019); DPIA completed before data collection.
- Pseudonymised study IDs; the linkage key is encrypted and held only by the data manager.
- REDCap hosted in-country with AES-256 encryption at rest, TLS in transit, role-based access and audit logs.
- The tablets store no identifiers, use full-disk encryption and can be wiped remotely.
- Data are retained for 5 years after study close and then destroyed securely. A de-identified dataset is shared on request under a data-use agreement.

## 7. Summary Information

### Impact Statement

If effective, TRIAGE-KE will offer county governments an affordable, offline-capable way to reduce dangerous under-triage in busy EDs. It will also produce one of the first African implementation datasets on human–AI collaboration in emergency care. The governance approach (advisory-only design, override logging, drift monitoring and DPIA) is intended as a reusable template for other AI health tools under Kenya's digital health regulations.

### Dissemination Plan

1. Two open-access peer-reviewed publications (effectiveness; implementation).
2. Presentations at a regional emergency medicine conference and a digital health conference (2027).
3. Dissemination workshops with the county health management teams in Kisumu, Nakuru and Machakos.
4. A policy brief for the Ministry of Health Digital Health directorate.
5. The model card, analysis code and de-identified data dictionary will be deposited in an open repository with a DOI.
